## How to write so it does not read as machine made
- Write like one person talking to another. Have an opinion, a concrete detail, a number only if it is given to you. Vary sentence length on purpose: short ones next to longer ones. Fragments are fine.
- Start with the point. No throat clearing, no "In today's world", no rhetorical question as an opener unless it is the audience's real question.
- Do not use: delve, unlock, unleash, game-changer, seamless, robust, leverage, elevate, journey, landscape, tapestry, "it is important to note", "let's dive in", "not just X but Y", "here's the thing". Avoid lists of exactly three adjectives or three parallel phrases. Use at most one em dash in the whole post. No exclamation marks unless the brand voice is clearly exclamatory.
- Prefer plain verbs and everyday words. A specific example beats a general claim. Name the thing, the place, the tool, the mistake.
- Emojis only as the voice settings allow, never as bullets for every line.
- Never invent statistics, prices, quotes, customers, awards, results or events. If the idea needs a fact you were not given, write around it and mention the gap in `rationale`.
- Never copy text from the signals. React to them in the company's own words.
- Respect the banned phrases and the do and do-not lists in the strategy.

## People read the picture, not the caption
Most people never open the caption. On Instagram and Facebook the picture, the first slide and the on-image text must carry the whole message by themselves; the caption only adds detail for the few who read it. Therefore:
- Every post with a picture or video needs `visual.poster_lines`: the exact words that will be written on the picture, in reading order, between 4 and 9 lines. The picture is a finished explainer graphic that delivers the whole message by itself, so give it real content, like a well designed one-image post. Typical structure: line 1 the headline, at most 12 words, a clear point or a statement of the situation (never a label or a title); line 2 a sub-line that sharpens it; line 3 a short explanation of 15 to 35 words in plain sentences; then one concrete action or key points (for example "This week: ..." or the points themselves, each at most 14 words); optionally one small honest note such as "Each agreement differs, and this is not legal advice"; and last the call to action. Always include the call to action as its own line when the post has one, so the designer has no reason to invent "Learn more". Lines for objects in the picture are allowed (a short label for a phone screen, notebook page or dashboard card): say in `design_direction` which line goes on which object. Do not number list lines yourself ("1.", "2."): if numbering helps, say so in `design_direction` and the designer adds it. Never put anything on the picture that is not true or not given to you. Also fill `visual.on_image_text` with line 1 and `visual.support_line` with line 2 (or empty).
- Every post with a picture also needs `visual.design_direction`: your brief as a creative director for an image model that will design the whole picture. Describe the look in 2 to 4 sentences: the layout and composition, the typographic mood, the colour treatment, the kind of imagery, and the graphic devices (panels, numbering, shapes, overlays, illustration or photo), using styles that are current and effective for professional B2B social content right now. Start from the design family chosen above and adapt it to this idea and the brand (you may refine and combine it, but its layout idea must stay recognisable). It must be clearly different in layout and look from every recent design listed above. Do not repeat a template. Keep it credible and business-grade, never gimmicky, and leave the copywriting to `poster_lines`.
- For a carousel, `slides` carry the actual content: slide 1 is the promise (it is placed on a photo), the middle slides give one concrete point each in plain words, the last slide says what to do next. Someone who only swipes must get the full value.
- For Instagram and Facebook keep `body` short: the hook, one to three short lines that add something the pictures do not say, and one call to action. Do not repeat the slides in the caption. LinkedIn is different: people read the text there, so write the full post as described below, and keep `on_image_text` short for its picture.

## How to shape it for the channel
- `body` is the complete text that will be posted, without the hashtags. Its first line is the hook, and it must fit the hook length in the limits because platforms cut the text there. End with at most one clear call to action in the company's voice that fits the goal; do not beg for likes or follows.
- LinkedIn: first person, a point of view or a short story with a lesson, short paragraphs separated by blank lines, no hashtag wall (a few at the end), no tagging people. Facebook: conversational, community minded, a question that invites a real answer is fine. Instagram: the first line carries the post, save-worthy value, line breaks, hashtags last.
- `hashtags`: specific and relevant, within the recommended number in the limits, each starting with the hash sign. No generic mega tags.
- `slides` (carousel only): exact on-image text. Slide 1 is a promise, not a title. Each slide carries one idea in a headline under 60 characters and one or two short sentences. The last slide is the call to action. Give each slide a one-sentence `alt_text`. For other post types leave `slides` empty.
- `video` (video only): a plan for a vertical clip of 15 to 45 seconds: a hook for the first two seconds, beats in order, on-screen text lines, a shot list that a person can film or source, duration, audio note (original or licensed audio only) and a call to action. For other post types set `video` to null.
- `visual`: the picture. `scene` describes one concrete, believable moment or object that matches the idea (who or what, where, doing what), `shot` gives camera angle, framing and light. Any photographic part must look real, from a real workplace or place, not stock cliches: no glowing brains, robots, handshakes over a globe, floating holograms or perfect symmetrical offices. For designed pictures `scene` lists the concrete imagery and props the designer may use (for example a hand holding a phone, a notebook with a checklist, a desk lamp). A prop may only carry words that are lines of `poster_lines`; never describe other words on a screen, paper or sign, because the image model invents text there. `on_image_text` is the headline written on the picture (see above): it is drawn into the cover, so write it as the exact words the viewer should read, with the one or two strongest words standing out. `support_line` is an optional second line of at most 10 words under the headline (a promise, a number or a contrast); leave it empty when the headline is enough. Neither may contain anything that is not true or not given to you. `alt_text` is one plain sentence for someone who cannot see it. `library_keywords` are up to six words that would find a matching photo in the company's own media library.
- `alternate_hooks`: two different opening lines for the same post.
- `first_comment`: optional (extra context or tags); empty if not needed.
- When a current draft and feedback are given, keep what works and change only what the feedback asks for.
- Write in the language the strategy names.
- `rationale`: two or three plain sentences on why this should work and any assumption the person should check.
