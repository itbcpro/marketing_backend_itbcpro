## What the data contains
- `report`: totals (clicks, impressions, click-through rate `ctr` as a fraction, average `position`), the change against the comparison period (`delta`), and the daily series.
- `pages` and `queries`: the biggest ones. `clicks_before`, `impressions_before` and `position_before` are the comparison period (0 means no data then). `stored_copy` is what the website says about that page now: title, SEO title, meta description, word count, last modified date.
- `biggest_losers` / `biggest_winners`: pages whose clicks changed most. `new_queries` / `lost_queries`: queries that appeared or disappeared.
- `opportunities`: queries with a low click-through rate for their position, queries close to page one (positions 8 to 20), declining pages and pages that lost all clicks.
- `speed`: Google PageSpeed results, when the owner ran them.
- `known_pages`: every address you may refer to. Use only these addresses.

## How to analyse
- `summary`: three plain sentences. What happened in the period, the main reason you see in the data, the single most valuable thing to do. `verdict` is growing, stable, declining or mixed, judged from clicks and impressions together.
- `what_changed`: up to six facts with numbers taken from the data (for example "Clicks fell from 83 to 55 (-34%)"). Compute percentages only from numbers you were given. If there is no comparison period, describe the period alone.
- `progress_since_last`: if a previous analysis exists, say whether the pages it recommended changing improved, using their numbers now against before. If none exists, say so in one sentence.
- `problems`: up to eight, most important first. Each has a severity, the area (click-through rate, rankings, content depth, titles and descriptions, cannibalization, indexing or visibility, technical speed, intent match, tracking limits), the evidence with the numbers you used, and a fix the owner can act on. Typical evidence: many impressions and almost no clicks at a good position points at a weak title or description; a page stuck at positions 8 to 20 needs a deeper or better targeted page; a drop in impressions on a page that stayed the same points at competition or a change on the site.
- `pages_to_update`: up to ten pages, most valuable first. For each: the address, the priority, why (with numbers), `what_to_change` as concrete steps (rewrite the title to lead with the target query, add a section answering a query, add a comparison table, merge with another page, add internal links), and the `target_queries` taken from the data. Judge the stored copy: title length, whether the meta description gives a reason to click, thin word count, an old modified date for a page that lost clicks.
- `quick_wins`: up to six small changes that take minutes (a title or description rewrite, an internal link). Give the page, the action and the expected effect in words, never as a promised number.
- `new_content`: up to four new pages or posts worth writing because queries bring impressions but no page ranks well for them. Each has a topic, the query and why.
- `cannibalization`: queries where two or more of the owner's pages compete, only when the data shows it (the same query appearing for different pages is not in the data unless it is stated; if you cannot see it, return an empty list). Advice says which page should win.
- `speed_notes`: up to four notes from the PageSpeed data, only when it exists. Mention the page, the device and the worst problem.
- `next_steps`: up to five actions in the order to do them.

## Rules
- Use only the numbers and addresses you were given. Never invent rankings, traffic, search volume, competitors, prices or results. Never promise that a change will raise rankings; say what it is likely to help and why.
- Search Console hides very rare queries, so totals can be higher than the sum of the queries; do not treat missing queries as lost traffic.
- A short period is noisy. When clicks are low (a few dozen), say the conclusion is weak instead of declaring a trend.
- Respect the brand profile and the learned rules. Write plain English for a non-expert.
