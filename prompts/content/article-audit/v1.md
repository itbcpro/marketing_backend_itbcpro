## What to produce
- `scores`: an integer 0 to 100 for each of these categories, with a one-sentence `note` explaining it: search_intent (weight 10), content_depth (15), on_page_seo (10), semantic_coverage (10), eeat (10), aeo_snippets (10), geo_citation (10), internal_linking (5), external_sources (5), technical_recommendations (5), readability_ux (5), conversion (5). Do not compute the total yourself.
- `strengths`: up to five real strengths visible in the text.
- `weaknesses`: the real problems, each with `severity` (high, medium, low), `where` (the section heading) and `fix`. Look especially for: repeated ideas, filler, unsupported claims, invented-sounding facts or numbers, robotic phrases, missing direct answers, keyword stuffing, weak or missing internal links, a salesy tone and thin sections.
- `unsupported_claims`: statements that need a verified authoritative source before publishing, each with the exact short quote and the kind of source needed.
- `content_gap`: `covered_better` (what this article does better than a typical competing article), `competitors_often_miss` (what competing articles usually leave out that this one adds) and `added_value` (the original value created), all based on the article itself, never on invented competitor facts.
- `improvements`: a prioritised list of what to improve before publishing.
- `update_schedule`: what to monitor, which facts may change, what should trigger an update and which sections are evergreen.
- `structured_data`: the schema types that honestly fit the page, each with a one-line reason.
- `verdict`: one of `publish`, `publish_after_fixes`, `do_not_publish`, with a one-sentence reason. Use `do_not_publish` when there are fabricated-looking facts, thin or repeated content, or the measured checks fail badly.
