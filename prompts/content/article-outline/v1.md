## What to produce
Think like a researcher first: the dominant search intent, who is asking and why, the questions people ask, what a typical competing article covers, what it leaves out, and the information gap this article will fill. Then plan.
- `intent` and `audience` in one sentence each; `information_gap` in two or three sentences naming what this article adds.
- `h1` (clear, specific, contains the primary keyword when natural), `seo_title` (about 50 to 60 characters, never over 65), `meta_description` (about 140 to 158 characters, never over 170, says what the reader gets), `slug` (short, lower case, hyphens, no dates).
- `key_takeaways`: three to five short, specific statements the article proves.
- `sections`: the body in order. Each is an H2 with `heading`, `purpose` (what the reader learns), `key_points` (the specific points, examples, numbers-to-verify and decisions to cover, as a list), `target_words`, `format` (prose, steps, table, checklist, comparison or mixed), `subheadings` (planned H3 titles, possibly empty), `direct_answer_question` (a question this section answers directly, or empty), `link_to` (addresses from the existing pages list that fit this section naturally, possibly empty). The first section is the introduction and the last is the conclusion with the next step; the introduction names the topic, answers the main question briefly and says what the article covers. Choose as many sections as the topic really needs. The `target_words` of all sections must add up to at least ${min_words} words, with the total about ten percent above that number; do not make the plan longer by splitting one point into several sections.
- `faq_questions`: at least 20 distinct questions real people ask (beginner, practical, cost, comparison, troubleshooting, misconceptions), no duplicates of the section questions.
- `entities`: the people, organisations, standards, products and concepts the article must name correctly.
- `needs_source`: every claim, number or study the article would need an authoritative source for. The writer will not state them as fact without a supplied source.
- `image_plan`: one featured image and three to six inline images or diagrams that help understanding. Each has `placement` (the word "featured", or exactly the heading text of one of your sections so the image can be placed there), `concept` (what it shows, concrete), `alt` (accurate, no keyword stuffing) and `caption`.
- `schema_types`: the structured data that honestly matches the page (for example BlogPosting, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, HowTo).
- `cta`: one natural call to action that fits the intent and the company.
- `faq_heading`: the heading of the FAQ section at the end, in the article's language.
- `freshness_notes`: what in the article will go out of date and how often to check it.
